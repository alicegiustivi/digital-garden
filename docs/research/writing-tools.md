# How writing tools organise projects, material and outlines

Research for issue #114 (part of the Express map, #113). Question: how do Scrivener, Ulysses, iA Writer, Obsidian with the Longform plugin, and Capacities organise **projects**, the **material** gathered for a piece, and **outlines**? What should the Garden borrow or avoid for **Pieces** and **Projects**?

Sources were read on 2026-11-10. Official docs, help centres and the vendors' own blogs are the primary source for every claim. Where the official page did not say something, this note says so. Two Scrivener points come from its official user forum and are marked as such.

## Summary

- **Every tool splits "the writing" from "the material".** Scrivener has a Draft folder and a Research folder. Ulysses has regular sheets and material sheets. Longform has scenes and ignored files. Only the writing goes out on export; material stays behind. The split is the one idea all five share.
- **Material beside the draft is always a second pane.** Scrivener's split editor and Copyholder, Ulysses' attached notes, Obsidian's split tabs and Capacities' side panel all do the same thing: the draft on one side, a source or note on the other. None of them merges material into the draft by itself.
- **An outline is an ordered list of parts, and the parts are the writing.** In Scrivener, Ulysses and Longform you reorder index cards, sheets or scenes, and the manuscript is rebuilt from that order. Capacities and iA Writer have no outline object: in Capacities you write an outline page and link a draft page to it; in iA Writer you join files with Content Blocks.
- **Where a piece stands is a label the writer sets.** Scrivener's Status ("To Do, In Progress, First Draft"), Capacities' status labels, and tags in Ulysses and iA Writer. None of them moves on its own. The task-manager drift comes from **goals and deadlines** (Ulysses, Longform) and from **tasks with dates** (Capacities), not from the label itself.
- **Quotes and citations are thin everywhere.** Scrivener hands citations to an outside reference manager. iA Writer has a citation syntax. Capacities' highlight blocks remember where they came from but the docs don't say what happens on export. No tool marks quoted text as "not the writer's words" when it is copied out, except iA Writer's **Authorship**, which marks AI and other people's text, and that is the closest match to the Garden's Origin rule.

## Tool by tool

### Scrivener

- **Project holds pieces and material.** A project's Binder has three root folders. **Draft** holds the manuscript; **Research** holds "anything that is not your manuscript but is pertinent to the writing process", such as PDFs, notes and images; **Trash** holds what you have set aside. [L&L knowledge base: The Three Root Folders](https://scrivener.tenderapp.com/help/kb/features-and-usage/the-three-root-folders). Research can hold "texts, PDFs, images, videos, and audio files", is searchable with the project, and is "not included when you compile". [L&L blog: Scrivener for non-fiction](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/blog/how-to-use-scrivener-for-writing-non-fiction)
- **Material beside the draft.** Background material opens "right next to your work"; the split editor shows "up to four different documents open in the same project window at once". [Scrivener overview](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/scrivener/overview). A **Copyholder** pins another item (research, a character sheet, notes) beside, above or below the document you are writing; it shows one document only. [L&L blog: Use Copyholders](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/blog/use-copyholders-to-extend-the-scrivener-editor), [Copyholders and Layouts](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/blog/scrivener-3-copyholders-and-layouts)
- **Outlines turn into writing.** On the **Corkboard** "every section of your project is attached to a virtual index card", each card carries a synopsis, and moving cards rearranges the manuscript. The **Outliner** shows the same parts as rows with synopses and word counts. [Scrivener overview](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/scrivener/overview). The outline and the draft are the same objects: there is nothing to convert.
- **Where it stands.** Three tools: custom icons, **Labels** (coloured tags, often used for draft stages such as red, yellow, green) and **Status** (text; defaults include "To Do, In Progress, First Draft"). Status can show as a stamp on each index card. [L&L blog: Three Ways to Mark the Status](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/blog/three-ways-to-mark-the-status-of-items-in-your-scrivener-project), [5 Tips for Using Labels](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/blog/5-tips-for-using-labels-in-scrivener). Scrivener also has word-count targets (not studied here).
- **Export.** **Compile** gathers the Draft into one document (Word, PDF, plain text and more). [Scrivener overview](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/scrivener/overview). Footnotes go at page bottoms or the end; you choose whether comments and annotations are included. [L&L blog: non-fiction](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/blog/how-to-use-scrivener-for-writing-non-fiction). Per the official forum, comments and annotations are removed by default at compile. [Forum: How to include annotations in compile](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/how-to-include-annotations-in-compile/127418)
- **Citations.** Scrivener ships templates for Chicago, APA and MLA [L&L blog: non-fiction](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/blog/how-to-use-scrivener-for-writing-non-fiction) but does not format citations itself. Per a community wiki on the official forum, you link an outside reference manager (Zotero, Bookends, EndNote), paste temporary citation placeholders while writing, and have the manager scan the compiled file to fill in real citations. [Forum wiki: Using Scrivener with reference software](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/using-scrivener-with-reference-software-wiki/132295). I could not read the user manual's own section (the PDF is too large to fetch), so treat this as forum-level.

### Ulysses

- **Project holds pieces and material.** "Each project is actually a standalone library": its writing lives in groups of sheets, with an **Extras** area for other items, which are left out of the project's export. [Ulysses help: Projects](https://help.ulysses.app/the-library/projects)
- **Material sheets.** A sheet can be marked as **material**, for "items that are not meant to become part of your final text": research, notes, rough ideas. A material sheet sits in the same group as the writing, is marked with "a thin red line", and is left out of statistics, export and writing goals. Unlike a note attached to one sheet, it can serve a whole group. [Ulysses blog: Material sheets](https://blog.ulysses.app/material-sheets/), [Ulysses help: Sheets & Groups](https://help.ulysses.app/the-library/567894-sheets-groups)
- **Material beside the draft.** **Notes** attach to a sheet as a whole and are "always excluded" from export. Inline **comments** (`++…++`) and **annotations** (`{…}`) sit in the text and are exported only by some export styles. [Ulysses help: Comments, Notes, Annotations](https://help.ulysses.app/en_US/dive-into-editing/comments-notes-annotations)
- **Outlines turn into writing.** Sheets sorted "Manually" can be dragged into order; a split sheet becomes two **glued** sheets that "behave as if they were one" in the editor and on export. Gluing works only with manual sorting. [Ulysses help: Sheets & Groups](https://help.ulysses.app/the-library/567894-sheets-groups). The order of sheets is the outline.
- **Where it stands.** Keywords on a sheet are used for things like stage, and live outside the text. [Ulysses blog: Attachments tips](https://blog.ulysses.app/attachments-tips/). **Goals** are where it drifts toward a task manager: a project or group goal can have a **Deadline**, and "Ulysses will automatically calculate how much you need to write each day to stay on track", with writing days set globally and a progress bar for today's portion. [Ulysses help: Goals](https://help.ulysses.app/goals)
- **Quotes and citations.** Footnotes (typed as `(fn)`) are numbered automatically on export. [Ulysses help: Comments, Notes, Annotations](https://help.ulysses.app/en_US/dive-into-editing/comments-notes-annotations). I found no citation feature in the help centre.

### iA Writer

- **Project holds pieces and material.** No project object. Writing is plain files in folders; the **Organizer** adds Favorites, **Smart Folders** (saved rules on path, search, kind and dates) and **Hashtags** (type `#word` anywhere; the Organizer lists every file containing it). [iA Writer: Organize](https://ia.net/writer/support/library/organize), [Stay Organized](https://ia.net/writer/how-to/stay-organized)
- **Material beside the draft.** The support pages list a live Preview, Document Outline and Focus Mode, but no side-by-side material pane. [iA Writer: Features](https://ia.net/writer/support/basics/features)
- **Outlines turn into writing.** **Content Blocks** pull another file into a document on a line of its own; "Content Blocks are popular with writers who need to compile the chapters of a book into a single document." The included text appears in Preview and in HTML, PDF, Markdown and Word exports. [iA Writer: Content Blocks](https://ia.net/writer/support/library/content-blocks)
- **Where it stands.** Only what you build from hashtags and smart folders; no stage field. [iA Writer: Organize](https://ia.net/writer/support/library/organize)
- **Authorship.** iA Writer 7 marks text pasted from an AI as AI text and shows it faded, while "your own words" are shown in black; as you rewrite, it "keeps track of what is yours and what isn't". It can also mark other human authors. The marks are kept in a block at the end of the plain-text file. The article argues the AI should be a dialogue partner, not a ghostwriter that makes "you lose your voice". It does not say what Authorship does on export. [iA: Authorship in Times of Artificial Intelligence](https://ia.net/topics/ia-writer-7)
- **Quotes and citations.** Block quotes use `>`. Footnotes use `[^1]` and appear at the end on export. Citations use a key such as `[p. 23][#Doe:2006]` (Mac, iPhone, iPad only); the guide does not say how they look on export. [iA Writer: Markdown Guide](https://ia.net/writer/support/basics/markdown-guide)

### Obsidian with Longform

- **Project holds pieces and material.** Longform finds a project by a `longform` entry in a note's frontmatter; that **index note** is the project's "spine". A multi-scene project lists its scenes in the index note; a single-scene project is one note. [Longform README](https://github.com/kevboh/longform). Scenes "live in the same folder". A file in that folder that is not a scene can be **ignored** (for example `*-scratch` notes): it stays beside the scenes but is not listed or compiled. That is the nearest thing to a material area; the docs don't talk about research. [Longform: Multiple Scene Projects](https://github.com/kevboh/longform/blob/main/docs/MULTIPLE_SCENE_PROJECTS.md)
- **Material beside the draft.** Plain Obsidian: split a tab right or down, or pin a tab so links open beside it. [Obsidian help: Tabs](https://obsidian.md/help/tabs)
- **Outlines turn into writing.** Scenes are "a manually-ordered set of notes", reordered by drag and drop and nested by indenting; the order is saved in the index note. [Longform: Multiple Scene Projects](https://github.com/kevboh/longform/blob/main/docs/MULTIPLE_SCENE_PROJECTS.md)
- **Where it stands.** No stage or status. Word counts per project, draft and scene, and timed writing **sessions** against a word-count goal that notify you when you hit it. [Longform: Word Counts](https://github.com/kevboh/longform/blob/main/docs/WORD_COUNTS.md). Several **drafts** of one project are grouped as versions. [Longform README](https://github.com/kevboh/longform)
- **Export.** **Compile** runs steps in order: per-scene steps, a join, then manuscript steps. Built-in steps include Strip Frontmatter, Remove Comments, **Remove Links** (wikilinks and external links, both on by default), Concatenate Text and Write to Note. The docs don't say how embeds are treated. [Longform: Compile](https://github.com/kevboh/longform/blob/main/docs/COMPILE.md). No citation handling.

### Capacities

- **Project holds pieces and material.** No writing project object. Everything is an Object of a type; projects are made from types, **labels** (a status label, for example "Ongoing"), tags, hand-picked **collections** and rule-based **queries**. [Capacities: Organizational structures](https://docs.capacities.io/reference/organizational-structures). For writing, "tags are more flexible than folders" and a page replaces a document. [Capacities for writing](https://docs.capacities.io/tutorials/capacities-for-writing)
- **Material beside the draft.** Shift-click opens an object in the **side panel**. The writing tutorial suggests "outline on left side, full draft on right side", or the draft beside resources found by Shift-clicking a tag; `@` links a resource into the draft. [Capacities for writing](https://docs.capacities.io/tutorials/capacities-for-writing), [Navigation](https://docs.capacities.io/reference/navigation)
- **Outlines turn into writing.** Write a bullet outline page, then create a linked "Full version" page from it, or keep the outline at the top of the draft page. [Capacities for writing](https://docs.capacities.io/tutorials/capacities-for-writing). The outline and draft are separate pages; nothing turns one into the other.
- **Where it stands.** Status labels on the type. [Organizational structures](https://docs.capacities.io/reference/organizational-structures). The GTD guide shows the drift: a project note has a status (In Progress, On Hold) and a **Tasks** tab of linked tasks, and tasks carry a **date** and a **deadline**. [Capacities: Getting Things Done](https://docs.capacities.io/reference/use-cases/getting-things-done), [Task Management](https://docs.capacities.io/reference/task-management). This is the productivity layer the owner left Capacities over (map #113, Out of scope).
- **Quotes and citations.** A text block can be shown as a quote. **Highlight blocks** (from Readwise, Kindle, web highlights) show quoted text with a coloured bar, record when and where the highlight was made, open the original location, and can become a standalone object that still links back. [Capacities: Highlight Blocks](https://docs.capacities.io/reference/highlight-blocks), [Blocks](https://docs.capacities.io/reference/blocks). The docs don't say how highlights look when copied or exported. No citation formatting.

## Comparison

| | Scrivener | Ulysses | iA Writer | Longform | Capacities |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Project object | yes (one file) | yes (a library) | no (folders) | yes (index note) | no (types + labels) |
| Writing vs material split | Draft / Research | sheets / material sheets | none | scenes / ignored files | none |
| Material beside the draft | split editor, Copyholder | attached notes | none | Obsidian split tabs | side panel |
| Outline = order of the parts | yes (corkboard, outliner) | yes (manual sort, glue) | via Content Blocks | yes (scene list) | no (separate outline page) |
| Stage set by the writer | Status, Label | keywords | hashtags | none | status label |
| Deadlines or tasks | targets (not studied) | goals with deadlines | Writing Goals (Windows) | session goals | tasks with date and deadline |
| Material left out of export | yes | yes | n/a | yes (ignored) | n/a |
| Comments left out of export | by default | depends on style; notes always | n/a | Remove Comments step | not documented |
| Citations | outside manager | none found | citation keys | none | none |
| Marks text that isn't the writer's | no | no | yes (Authorship) | no | highlights only |

## What this means for the Garden

In the Garden's words: a **Piece** is like a Scrivener Draft document or a Ulysses sheet, a **Project** is like their project, and the Garden's existing Objects connected to a Project play the part of Research and material sheets. The Garden already has two things none of these tools has: **Origin** on every block of text, and **Quotes** that keep a Mention of their Source. Those carry most of the citation job.

### Borrow

1. **Material stays material.** Like Scrivener's Research folder and Ulysses' material sheets, the Objects connected to a Project or Piece never go out when a Piece is copied or exported. Only the Piece's body does.
2. **Material beside the Piece, one at a time.** A side pane showing one connected Object (Source, Thought, Concept) next to the Piece while the owner writes, like Scrivener's Copyholder or Capacities' side panel. The owner reads it and writes in their own words; nothing is pasted in for them.
3. **The order of Pieces is the Project's outline.** A Project shows its Pieces in an order the owner drags, like Scrivener's corkboard and Longform's scene list. Reordering is the only "outline" tool; no separate outline object to convert.
4. **Stage as a stamp.** Show a Piece's Stage (Idea, Drafting, Finished) as a small stamp on its card, like Scrivener's status stamps, set only by the owner and never changed by the app.
5. **Notes about a Piece are not part of it.** Like Ulysses' notes ("always excluded"), AI Notes and the owner's side notes on a Piece never travel with the copied or exported text.
6. **Quotes stay marked on the way out.** Copy or export a Piece with each Quote as a block quote followed by its Source's title and link, using what the Quote's Mention already knows. This is more than any of the five tools does, and costs nothing.
7. **Show what is yours.** iA Writer's Authorship (own words plain, other text faded) supports the Garden's Origin rule: in a Piece, the owner's words plain, Quotes marked, and nothing from the AI at all.

### Avoid

1. **Goals, deadlines and "words per day"** (Ulysses' goal deadlines, Longform's session goals). They are the step from writing tool to task manager, and the map rules them out.
2. **Tasks with dates on a Project** (Capacities' Tasks tab with date and deadline). That is the trap the owner left Capacities over.
3. **A Project that is a folder** (Scrivener's Binder, iA Writer's folders). In the Garden an Object can feed several Projects; material should be connected, not moved inside.
4. **Hand-built status from tags or hashtags** (iA Writer, Ulysses keywords). The Garden has a real Stage; don't make the owner invent one.
5. **Stripping links on export by default** (Longform's Remove Links). For the Garden, a Quote's link to its Source is the citation; keep it, just turn it into plain text the owner can paste.
6. **Outside reference managers and citation styles** (Scrivener's Zotero round trip, iA Writer's citation keys). Too much setup for a non-programmer, and the Garden's Sources already hold what a simple credit line needs.
7. **The AI filling material into the draft** in any form. All five tools keep material in a second pane; the Garden should too, and the AI's help stays in AI Notes beside the Piece.
